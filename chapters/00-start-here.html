Know exactly what you're walking into: the format, the scoring, and what the five domains cover.

N10-009Current version. Launched June 2024, expected to retire around 2027.
≤ 90Questions: multiple-choice plus performance-based (PBQs)
90 minThat's about 1 minute per question. PBQs take longer.
720Passing score on a 100–900 scale. That's roughly 80%, but the exact cutoff isn't published.
  • Every question maps to an objective. The chapters here follow CompTIA's objective numbers (1.1 … 5.5), so you always know which objective you're on.
  • PBQs come first. These are drag-and-drop or simulation tasks: match ports, place cables, fix a firewall rule, pick the right troubleshooting step. They're worth more than one MCQ each.
  • Questions are scenarios. They ask what to do "BEST", "FIRST" or "MOST likely", not just for definitions. Knowing the why wins.
  • No penalty for guessing. Never leave a question blank.
Where to focus

Troubleshooting (24%) + Concepts (23%) = almost half the exam. Subnetting (1.7), ports (1.4), and tools and commands (5.5) appear again and again.

  1. Book the exam. Pick a date on or before Mon Nov 30 at comptia.org (online or at a Pearson VUE test center). Then enter that date on the plan page so the countdown is right.
  2. Take the baseline. 20 mixed questions → Don't study first. A low score is expected and useful, because it shows where to spend your time.

Set up a study routine that works with ADHD instead of against it.

  1. Start tiny
    "Open the next lesson" counts as starting. Momentum does the rest.
  2. 25 on / 5 off
    Click the timer button in the top bar. When it rings, stand up.
  3. Active, not passive
    Pause the video and say the answer out loud before the narrator does.
  4. Mark progress
    The ✓ is the reward. Let yourself enjoy it.
  • One lesson at a time. The guide shows one open lesson and one Next button, so you never have to decide what to do next.
  • Watch at 1.25–1.5×. Messer speaks slowly and clearly. Faster playback keeps your attention from drifting.
  • Body doubling helps. Professor Messer's free monthly study group replays and his Discord give you people to study "with".
  • Phone in another room during a 25-minute block. Use this laptop's timer instead.
  • Bad day? Do the minimum: 10 subnet drills and 10 flashcards. Then you haven't broken the chain.
